Why this document exists

The OpsTrax telematics screens read live database tables — there is no seeded or fabricated data behind them. A field with no reading renders as — rather than inventing a number. That honesty is deliberate, and it has a consequence: until real hardware reports, those screens stay empty by design.

Closing that gap needs a producer — something actually sending vehicle data. There are two routes, and this pack lists exactly what each one requires from you.

Two routes — pick one

RouteWhat it isYou provideLead time
A · Provider API Your existing fleet telematics account (Samsara, Motive, Geotab). We poll their API and forward into OpsTrax. API token, organisation/group ID, read scopes Days. No hardware, no vehicle access.
B · Direct hardware Trackers fitted to vehicles reporting through a protocol gateway into our signed ingest endpoint. Devices, SIMs, vehicles, workshop access Weeks. Needs physical fitting.

If you already run Samsara, Motive or Geotab, choose A. It is faster, needs no vehicle downtime, and gives richer data — engine RPM, fuel, diagnostic trouble codes — than a bare GPS tracker. Route B exists for fleets with no incumbent provider, or where you want OpsTrax reading hardware directly.

On VIN validation

VINs are checked rather than merely stored, so a mistyped character is caught at entry instead of silently corrupting vehicle identity later. If a VIN is rejected, it is worth re-reading from the door plate or V5 — the check digit is doing its job.

IMEI is not a credential

An IMEI identifies a device; it never authorises one. Packets are accepted only when signed with a secret held by the gateway, so a known IMEI alone cannot inject data into your fleet.

Network and endpoint configuration

Trackers in the PT40 / GT06 class speak a compact binary protocol, not HTTPS. A protocol gateway sits between the tracker and OpsTrax, decoding frames and forwarding them as signed JSON.

PathEndpointAuthentication
Gateway POST /api/telemetry/gps-ingest Headers X-Gateway-Id, X-Gateway-Timestamp (Unix seconds), X-Gateway-Signature — hex HMAC-SHA256 of <timestamp>.<raw-json>.
Native device POST /api/telemetry/ingest Per-device HMAC-SHA256 over X-Device-Key, X-Timestamp, X-Nonce, X-Signature.

Both paths enforce timestamp freshness and replay rejection, so a captured packet cannot be resent. Devices and vehicles are resolved strictly within your tenant.

The tracker itself needs only two settings from you: the APN for its SIM, and the destination host and port of the gateway, which we issue.

Secret handling

Non-negotiable

The gateway secret is at least 32 random characters, issued once, and stored encrypted. It is never written to the tracker, placed in a URL, printed to logs, or committed to source control.

Each secret is bound to one gateway and one tenant, and may only submit data for devices belonging to that tenant. If a secret is ever exposed, tell us and we rotate it — rotation is supported without re-fitting hardware.

If you have no hardware

We can source trackers ourselves. Rather than name a model before confirming availability in your market, here is the specification any candidate device must meet — we will confirm the exact unit at order.

  • Exposes a readable IMEI and manufacturer serial on the device label
  • Supports a configurable APN and a configurable destination host and port
  • Reports a position fix at 60-second intervals or better while moving
  • Speaks a documented protocol we can decode — PT40 / GT06 class or equivalent
  • For OBD/J1939 surfaces: reads engine PIDs — RPM, coolant, fuel level, fault codes
  • For cold chain: a temperature probe reporting on the same channel
  • Operates at the vehicle's voltage, 12 V or 24 V

Tell us the country of operation and we will confirm carrier and SIM options alongside the device, since APN and roaming behaviour vary by market.

How a device is bound to a vehicle

Worth understanding before fitting, because it determines what we record on the day.

Because the binding is dated rather than a simple pointer, moving a tracker from one vehicle to another does not rewrite history. Events recorded before the move stay attributed to the original vehicle. The database refuses overlapping installations for the same device, and refuses two primary devices of the same role on one vehicle — so a mis-fit is rejected at the point of entry rather than discovered later in a report.

Commissioning sequence

  1. Register

    Device recorded with its real IMEI, serial, model and firmware. Status opens at Provisioning.

  2. Fit

    Device installed on the vehicle. We record role, location on vehicle, odometer, and who fitted it.

  3. Configure

    APN and destination set on the tracker. Gateway issued its secret and pointed at the fleet.

  4. First signed packet

    The vehicle is driven. We watch for one genuine signed fix arriving end-to-end.

  5. Verify and sign off

    Status advances to Active only on that valid packet. Evidence pack issued to you.

Acceptance criteria

What does not count as proof

A device appearing in the registry is not proof it works. Neither is simulated movement on a map. A device is called live only when a valid signed packet has been observed end-to-end — and states that are suspended, revoked, inactive or unknown fail closed rather than being treated as working.

What you get back

On completion we issue an evidence pack per vehicle: the device identity as registered, the installation record with fitter and odometer, the first accepted packet with its timestamp, the observed road-test track, and the pass or fail result of every check above. Failures are reported as failures — a check that did not pass is never signed off as passed.

Sending this back

Reply with the route you have chosen and the tables filled in. For Route A that is the six API items and nothing more. For Route B it is the vehicle rows, device rows, and a proposed fitting window.

Anything you are unsure of — leave blank rather than guessing. A blank field we can chase; a wrong VIN or IMEI is rejected at registration and costs a return visit to the depot.
